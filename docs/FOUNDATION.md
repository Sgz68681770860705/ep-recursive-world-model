## 1. Overall Goal

The goal is not to assemble existing physical equations inside the model, nor to prescribe light, electrons, gravity, quantum mechanics, quaternions, or Hilbert space in advance.

The goal is to begin with as few underlying premises as possible and investigate whether the following can emerge layer by layer:

1. a statistical background corresponding to space;
2. propagation modes;
3. phase-transition objects;
4. mass, motion, and inertia;
5. forces and fields;
6. quantum probability and Hilbert-space structure;
7. recursive hierarchies;
8. and ultimately a computable and falsifiable form of **generative axiomatic physics**.

Core attitude:

- Do not write the desired results into the axioms in advance.
- Do not reject a deeper model merely because “existing physics says otherwise.”
- The experimentally established facts of existing physics must still serve as the final output gate.
- Elegance cannot replace derivation, but it can guide the search for minimal rules.
- Every use of “must” or “inevitably” must be classified as one of the following: logical necessity, model-internal necessity, high statistical probability, or empirical agreement.

## 2. Hierarchy and Basic Terminology

### 2.1 The S-1 Layer

S-1 is a foundational layer deeper than the observable S0 layer.

Provisional picture:

- S-1 constituents possess extremely high intrinsic motion speeds.
- They continuously move, collide, and exchange momentum.
- In a homogeneous region, their directional distribution rapidly approaches statistical isotropy.
- Mean momentum may be zero while momentum flux and pressure remain nonzero.
- The speed of an individual S-1 constituent may exceed the propagation speed of observable light-like modes at the S0 layer.
- “Individual speed exceeding collective-mode speed” is not by itself a logical contradiction, just as particle speed in a medium and wave speed are not the same concept.
- Whether controllable faster-than-light information can be transmitted must be tested separately. It cannot be assumed in advance.

### 2.2 The S0 Layer

S0 is the smallest observable phase-transition constituent maintained by the aggregation and locking of S-1 constituents together with continuous in/out exchange.

Provisional properties of S0:

- It has finite volume.
- It is the smallest observable carrier of energy–momentum.
- Under the same S-1 environmental density, S0 objects of the same type have the same size and energy.
- It is not a completely closed hard sphere, but a dynamically maintained phase-transition steady state.
- The specific S-1 members inside it may be continually replaced.
- The identity of the object belongs to its stable boundary, momentum distribution, and in/out statistical structure—not to fixed member labels.
- The region surrounding S0 remains filled with the S-1 background.
- The S0 boundary modifies S-1 incidence, emission, local density, fluctuation spectra, and momentum flux.

### 2.3 S1 and Higher Layers

Under suitable conditions, multiple S0 objects may form a stable higher-level phase-transition object S1.

A higher-level object is eligible to be defined only if it satisfies conditions such as:

- finite scale;
- fast internal mixing and slow external response;
- an identity lifetime much longer than the internal collision time;
- an external description requiring only a small number of state variables;
- approximate external equivalence among different internal microstates;
- the ability to define new in/out behavior, boundaries, momentum, and collision rules.

Recursion is not a presupposed axiom. It is a result to be discovered:

```text
Many lower-level degrees of freedom
→ Stable phase-transition object
→ External equivalence class
→ New effective object
→ Test whether its dynamics become isomorphic to the lower-level dynamics after rescaling
```

## 4. In/Out Dynamic Equilibrium Is Central

Even when an S0 or a higher-level phase-transition object is macroscopically at rest, a large amount of high-speed S-1 motion and exchange may continue internally.

Rest means only that:

- total momentum is statistically balanced;
- net constituent flux is zero;
- net energy flux is zero;
- the boundary and macroscopic form remain stable.

It does **not** mean that:

- internal members stop moving;
- no S-1 constituents enter;
- no S-1 constituents leave.

A stable phase-transition object should satisfy:

```text
J_in  ≠ 0
J_out ≠ 0
```

while, over long-time averages:

```text
net change in membership = 0
net change in energy     = 0
net change in total momentum = 0
```

A state of motion is also a form of in/out equilibrium, rather than an object continually pushing through a medium while permanently fighting drag.

## 5. Motion, Momentum, Velocity, and Inertia

### 5.1 Momentum Bias

Suppose a phase-transition object contains 10,000 S-1 constituents:

- 2,000 moving right;
- 1,000 moving left;
- the remaining 7,000 having a vector sum of zero.

Then the structure has a net momentum bias to the right.

However, this directly establishes only a **total momentum state**. It does not directly determine velocity.

### 5.2 A Forbidden Incorrect Derivation

One must not write:

```text
velocity = (net right-moving count / total count) × S-1 speed
```

because:

- the phase boundary carries momentum;
- the near-field environment carries momentum;
- binding energy contributes to total energy;
- in/out members are continuously replaced;
- the relation P = MV has not yet been derived;
- inertia must not be inserted in advance.

### 5.3 Correct Order of Reasoning

```text
Directional occupation imbalance
→ Define the total momentum P of the phase-transition object
→ Solve for moving stable solutions
→ Obtain the energy–momentum relation E(P)
→ Derive velocity from the translation rate of the stable solution or from dE/dP
→ Derive inertia from the momentum cost of changing the stable state of motion
```

### 5.4 Definition of Uniform Motion

Uniform motion is not “continuous drag plus continuous energy replenishment.”

Uniform motion is:

> a front–back asymmetric but globally balanced moving fixed point between a phase-transition object and space.

In a comoving coordinate frame, its internal statistical structure remains unchanged.

### 5.5 So-Called “Aether Drag”

Enhanced incidence from the front should not automatically be called drag.

One must distinguish between:

- the in/out anisotropy that defines the moving state;
- a residual imbalance that actually causes total momentum to decrease with time.

Only the latter is dissipative drag.

Velocity may be a first-order statistical projection of such directional in/out distributions.

## 6. Collision Statistics, Pressure, and Force

### 6.1 Homogeneous Background

In a homogeneous S-1 background:

- collisions are directionally symmetric on average;
- the total momentum flux is equal around an isolated boundary;
- therefore the net force is zero.

### 6.2 Boundary Effects

An S0/S1 boundary is not a passive hard shell. It can:

- absorb;
- lock;
- rearrange;
- reflect;
- release S-1 constituents.

When two boundaries approach one another, they jointly modify the intermediate region’s:

- allowed incidence directions;
- emission directions;
- local density;
- correlations and fluctuation spectra;
- momentum-flux tensor.

Therefore the pressures on different sides need not remain equal.

### 6.3 Unified Expression of Force

Force is not a pre-existing “line of attraction.”

Force is the statistical imbalance of momentum flux across a boundary.

Macroscopic attraction or repulsion is the result of a difference between internal-side and external-side collision statistics.

### 6.4 Inspiration from Casimir-Like Effects in Liquids

In real liquids, boundaries can restrict medium fluctuations, molecular arrangement, and electromagnetic response, producing effects such as:

- attraction;
- repulsion;
- oscillatory potentials;
- critical Casimir forces;
- solvation forces;
- hydration forces.

This demonstrates that:

```text
boundaries alter the allowed states and fluctuation spectra of a medium
→ effective forces can arise
```

This supports the logical route of the model, but it does not prove that all boundary effects are gravity.

Interactions in water are diverse because molecular speeds, roughness, electromagnetic bonding, surface chemistry, and fluctuation scales are complex.

### 6.5 Gravity Candidate

Gravity in this model is not a prior attractive force.

A candidate mechanism is:

- boundaries modify the incidence/emission statistics of S-1;
- two objects jointly create a momentum-flux deficit in the region between them;
- outside pressure exceeds inside pressure;
- external collisions push the objects toward one another.

If far-field shielding or response dilutes with solid angle in three-dimensional space, an inverse-square candidate may appear.

However, to qualify as real gravity, the mechanism must still demonstrate:

- long range;
- no screening or extremely weak screening;
- approximate inverse-square behavior;
- proportionality to total energy/mass;
- the equivalence principle;
- very low drag;
- very low heating;
- universal coupling;
- the ability to explain light bending and related phenomena.

## 7. Model of Light

### 7.1 Light in Vacuum

Light is not a collection of fixed S0 objects traveling unchanged from emitter to receiver.

Light is a directed, coherent propagation mode in the S0/S-1 background whose members are continually replaced.

What propagates is:

- momentum direction;
- phase;
- organizational form;
- a collision-relay pattern.

Fixed members do not need to travel all the way from source to destination.

### 7.2 Structural Analogy Between Light and Sound

Light and sound may be structurally analogous at the level of relay propagation:

```text
local state
→ transferred to forward members
→ rear members leave
→ the mode propagates forward
```

Their specific degrees of freedom need not be the same:

- sound is usually a longitudinal density/pressure mode;
- light must ultimately explain transverse behavior and two polarizations.

### 7.3 Speed of Light

The speed of light is understood as:

> the characteristic propagation speed of a light-like collective mode at the S0 layer in a particular background.

It need not equal the individual speed of S-1, nor must it be the absolute speed limit of every layer.

### 7.4 Why Light Slows in Matter

When a material such as glass changes the local S0/S-1 environment—its:

- effective density;
- collision update rate;
- residence time;
- response stiffness;
- in/out reconstruction process;

—the effective propagation speed of the light-like mode may decrease.

The observed arrival-time delay through glass is a basic fact the model must recover.

A careful statement is:

```text
The medium changes the relay time and response structure of the propagation mode
→ group velocity decreases
→ the light pulse arrives later
```

At present, one should not write only “greater density necessarily means lower speed,” because propagation speed may also depend on response stiffness and resonance structure.

### 7.5 Frequency and Intensity

Provisional distinction:

- **frequency**: the phase-update rate or internal coherent-update rate of a single propagation mode;
- **single-photon energy**: related to coherent occupancy and phase frequency;
- **intensity**: the total energy statistics of light-like events arriving per unit area per unit time.

Local energy density cannot simply be identified with frequency, because focusing can increase density without changing frequency.

### 7.6 Redshift

Density gradients, background changes, or coherence leakage may influence frequency.

One must distinguish among:

- speed change;
- wavelength change;
- amplitude attenuation;
- reduction in photon count;
- lowering of single-photon central frequency.

These are not interchangeable.

## 8. Phase-Transition Objects, Mass, and Matter Particles

### 8.1 Light-Like States and Matter-Like States

Light-like state:

Matter-like state:

- crosses a phase-transition threshold;
- forms a closed or quasi-closed boundary;
- maintains in/out dynamic equilibrium;
- has long lifetime;
- possesses rest energy;
- appears externally as a stable object.

### 8.2 Candidate Origin of Mass

Mass is not presupposed as an independent property.

It may emerge from:

- the phase-transition free-energy difference;
- a stable internal period;
- the momentum cost required to change a moving fixed point;

### 8.3 Naming Neutrinos, Electrons, and Other Particles

At the current stage, one should speak only of candidates such as:

- a neutral massive phase-transition object candidate;

## 10. The Proper Role of Hilbert Space

### 10.1 Hilbert Space Is Not the Fundamental Physical Container

Physical collisions occur in a real configuration space or phase space.

A Hilbert space is:

> a state space of square-integrable functions over all possible microscopic states.

### 10.2 The First Naturally Arising Hilbert Representation

Let the complete underlying configuration be denoted by `Z`.

Let the set of all possible configurations be `Ω`.

Define:

```text
H = L²(Ω, dμ)
```

The inner product represents overlap between state functions.

If the underlying evolution is:

- reversible;
- measure preserving;

then it induces a unitary evolution operator on `H`.

This first yields a **Hilbert representation of a classical dynamical system**, not full quantum mechanics.

### 10.3 Effective Hilbert Space of Phase-Transition Objects

Long-lived phase-transition objects may be treated as slow modes, metastable sets, or external equivalence classes in the underlying state space.

Their effective state space may be generated by these slow modes.

### 10.4 The Bridge from Classical Hilbert Space to Quantum Hilbert Space Still Requires Derivation

The model must additionally derive:

- physical superposition;
- complex-phase interference;
- the Born rule;
- noncommuting observables;
- tensor-product composition;
- entanglement;
- Bell correlations.

The existence of a Hilbert space does not mean that quantum mechanics has already been derived.

### 10.5 Collision and Inner Product Are Different Concepts

- **Collision**: an evolution map or operator.
- **Inner product**: overlap between states.
- **Integration**: statistical aggregation and expectation.
- A collision is not a dot product.
- Collision statistics are not themselves an inner product.

## 11. The Proper Role of Quaternions

Correct order:

```text
First discover the internal modes

```

Quaternions are not a universal foundational explanation, and they do not automatically generate spin-1/2, Fermi statistics, or quantum measurement rules.

## 19. Most Concise Current Statement of the Theory

The deepest layer of the universe may consist of one type of rapidly moving constituent governed by local collisions.

Homogeneous space is the statistically isotropic background formed by those constituents.

Local constituents may aggregate, lock, and maintain continuous in/out exchange to form finite phase-transition objects.

Propagation, motion, mass, inertia, and force are not properties assigned in advance. They may instead correspond to:

- **Propagation**: spatial relay of an organized collision pattern.
- **Motion**: a directional in/out moving fixed point between a phase-transition object and space.
- **Mass**: the rest energy of a stable phase-transition object and the momentum cost of changing its moving fixed point.
- **Inertia**: the momentum required to reconstruct the entire in/out equilibrium.
- **Force**: a statistical imbalance of momentum flux across boundaries.
- **Field**: scalar, vector, and tensor statistical moments of the underlying collision distribution.
- **Quantum probability**: a statistical projection of hidden deep-level states through finite-scale observation channels.
- **Hilbert space**: a function space over all possible underlying states and long-lived slow modes.
- **Recursion**: compression of stable phase-transition objects into higher-level effective objects.
- **Symmetry**: preservation of the form of a stable collision distribution under state transformations.

The minimal objective is not to prove the entire universe immediately, but to find a sufficiently simple underlying update rule from which the following can arise without specialized patches:

```text
homogeneous background
→ in/out phase-transition object
→ moving fixed point
→ weakly dissipative propagation mode
→ boundary statistical force
→ slow-mode Hilbert structure
→ hierarchical recursion
```
